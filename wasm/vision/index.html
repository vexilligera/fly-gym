Coverage and limits of this experiment

Simulated

Two body-attached eye cameras use NeuroMechFly’s field of view, fisheye correction, and 721 ommatidia per eye. R1–6 input events enter all 138,639-neuron / 15,091,983-connection FlyWire v783 dynamics. Spikes in downstream L2 cells steer the body through an engineered decoder. MuJoCo simulates the joint-actuated body, floor contact, and adhesion.

The arena is deliberately simple: one dark vertical target, a uniform background, and no competing obstacles. Self-body geometry is hidden from the eye cameras. Trial arrival means entering a 3 mm zone; the arena position only supplies rendering and this stopping criterion.

Engineered assumptions

The bundled annotations have no retinal registration. L2 cells are assigned synthetic pixel locations, and R1–6 cells follow their strongest released L2 connection. Neuron anchor positions are never treated as retinal directions. The map is reproducible, not a measured optic-lobe retinotopy.

Real photoreceptors signal through graded potentials and histamine release. Here dark contrast becomes Poisson input to uniform LIF neurons. Released synaptic signs are preserved, including many excitatory R1–6 outputs. This is not a validated photoreceptor-to-lamina model. Molecular chemistry, receptor binding, adaptation, plasticity, learning, memory, navigation planning, and a reconstructed VNC are absent.

Disconnecting eyes or shuffling the synthetic registration tests this engineered loop. Success does not validate the biological mapping or show that natural visual navigation emerged from the connectome.
