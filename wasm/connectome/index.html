Chemicals: what is actually modeled?

Odors: the odor control stimulates the 68 annotated ORN_DM1 sensory neurons. Exposure 0–1 maps to 0–150 input events/s by an illustrative rule. Resulting spikes propagate through the connectome. Odor stimulation is not guaranteed to produce walking.

Neurotransmitters: the released connection table supplies excitatory/inhibitory signs and synapse counts. These produce positive/negative postsynaptic input. There are no simulated molecules, receptor-binding reactions, drug doses, metabolism, or slow neuromodulatory pathways.

Coverage, sources, and limitations

This loads all 138,639 neurons and 15,091,983 connection rows in Eon’s public FlyWire v783 model, representing a synapse-count sum of 54,492,922. It is the released brain model, not a full brain-plus-VNC connectome.

Each neuron has the same simple leaky integrate-and-fire dynamics: threshold, reset, refractory period, synaptic decay, and delay. Morphology, detailed ion channels, receptor subtypes, plasticity, learning, and molecular chemistry are absent. The body uses position actuators and recorded step patterns.

The spatial view places 138,625 cells at their released annotation anchors. It converts 4 × 4 × 40 nm voxels to micrometers and starts in the FlyWire X / −Y plane, with −Z depth. No spatial positions are invented for the 14 unmatched cells. Colors mark spikes, not membrane voltage, calcium fluorescence, or chemical concentration. Amber cells receive direct input; teal cells are other firing neurons. Synaptic edges are simulated but not drawn.

Brain and body advance together in 20 ms batches. The view displays every firing neuron with coordinates in each completed batch; it does not resolve individual spike times inside that bin or interpolate activity. Pause holds the last bin. No neural state is prerecorded. This experiment is driven by external neural/odor stimulation; body sensations and vision are not fed back into the brain.
