What this simulation covers — and its limits

What runs

The branching maze has 25 connected cells, five dead ends, and eight turns on its route to sugar. The original two-baffle layout is also available. MuJoCo simulates the joint-actuated fly, leg contacts, adhesion, and solid maze walls. Two body-attached eye cameras produce 721 samples each. Two virtual antenna sensors sample the food-odor field at the fly’s current location. Both inputs enter one 138,639-neuron, 15,091,983-connection FlyWire v783 LIF simulation.

The odor field solves steady two-dimensional diffusion with decay and no flow through walls. It is pre-equilibrated, normalized, and spread around openings. It models an associated food scent, not airborne sucrose. Turning the source off starts a trial with zero odor everywhere.

The map and source coordinates are used to render the world, sample local sensors, and score arrival. The movement policy has no access to them. All plotted activation comes from modeled spikes.

What is approximated or absent

Retinal registration is synthetic. Real graded photoreceptor signals and histamine chemistry are replaced with contrast-driven Poisson inputs. Odor encoding uses a saturating response and a hand-set 16× bilateral contrast gain before stimulating 68 DM1 ORNs. This exaggerates small antenna differences and is not a validated receptor model.

Neuron readouts (default) use bilateral DNp09 for forward drive, MDN for reverse drive, and the DNa02 left/right difference for turning. Rates are smoothed over 100 ms and mapped to leg gains with fixed, uncalibrated scales. No baseline walking or sensory steering rule is added. The recorded-step leg CPG still supplies motor patterns. The optional sensory policy retains the earlier L2/ORN steering rules for comparison. Neither mode contains a reconstructed VNC; successful movement alone would not validate natural navigation.

Sugar presentation adds imposed input to 21 released sugar GRNs and observes MN9 motor-neuron spikes through the full connectome. The assay resets the brain and uses a separate clock shared with a three-hinge MuJoCo mouth model. A staged feeding pose faces the sugar-solution drop; the recorded navigation arrival is retained separately. An approximate labellum point inside the solution gates taste input. Mean MN9 activity drives rostrum extension; left/right imbalance drives a bounded yaw, and the haustellum follows a coupled target. These are engineered, uncalibrated servos with gravity compensation and no solid-mouth collision or fluid forces. Contact feeds back to taste input, but the solution is not depleted. There is no ingestion, pumping, receptor binding, hunger, satiety, learning, reward chemistry, or reconstructed VNC. Sleep dynamics are not modeled. The odor field has no wind, turbulent plume, or vertical transport. Neuron readouts can remain silent, move weakly, or send inappropriate commands; the model has no spontaneous drive, calibrated motor physiology, or body sensory feedback into the brain. The comparison policy can stall too. Neither mode plans routes or remembers dead ends.
