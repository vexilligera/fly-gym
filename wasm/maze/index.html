What this simulation covers — and its limits

What runs

The branching maze has 25 connected cells, five dead ends, and eight turns on its route to sugar. The original two-baffle layout is also available. MuJoCo simulates the joint-actuated fly, leg contacts, adhesion, and solid maze walls. Two body-attached eye cameras produce 721 samples each. Two virtual antenna sensors sample the food-odor field at the fly’s current location. Both inputs enter one 138,639-neuron, 15,091,983-connection FlyWire v783 LIF simulation.

The odor field solves steady two-dimensional diffusion with decay and no flow through walls. It is pre-equilibrated, normalized, and spread around openings. It models an associated food scent, not airborne sucrose. Turning the source off starts a trial with zero odor everywhere.

The map and source coordinates are used to render the world, sample local sensors, and score arrival. The movement policy has no access to them. All plotted activation comes from modeled spikes.

What is approximated or absent

Retinal registration is synthetic. Real graded photoreceptor signals and histamine chemistry are replaced with contrast-driven Poisson inputs. Odor encoding uses a saturating response and a hand-set 16× bilateral contrast gain before stimulating 68 DM1 ORNs. This exaggerates small antenna differences and is not a validated receptor model.

A hand-designed decoder combines downstream L2 activity and odor-receptor activity, with a constant walking drive and a recorded-step CPG. Downstream DM1 projection neurons are monitored but do not control steering. Success does not show that natural multisensory navigation emerged from the connectome.

There is no wind, turbulent plume, vertical odor transport, molecule-specific receptor binding, adaptation, learning, hunger, taste, feeding, metabolism, or reconstructed VNC. The sugar is a visual marker and arrival zone. The controller is reactive and can stall in the harder maze; it does not plan routes or remember dead ends.
